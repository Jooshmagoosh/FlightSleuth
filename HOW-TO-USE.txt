HOW TO USE FLIGHTSLEUTH
=======================

Flightsleuth finds cheap flights. You tell it where you want to fly and
roughly when, and it shows you the cheapest days and flights.

It is a normal window with boxes to fill in and a Search button.

Take your time. You cannot break the computer by clicking the wrong thing.
If something goes wrong, a small message box tells you what, and you can
try again.


PART 1: GET YOUR FREE KEY (you only do this once)
=================================================

The flight prices come from a service called SerpApi. It needs to know who
you are, so it gives you a "key", which is a long jumble of letters and
numbers that works like a password.

  1. Open your web browser (the program you use to look at websites).
  2. Go to:  serpapi.com
  3. Click "Register" and make a free account. You will need an email
     address. The free account gives you 250 searches every month.
  4. Once you are signed in, go to:  serpapi.com/manage-api-key
  5. You will see your key. Click the copy button next to it.

Keep this key private. Do not share it with anyone.


PART 2: OPEN FLIGHTSLEUTH
=========================

  1. On the keyboard, hold down the Windows key (it has the little window
     symbol, bottom left) and press the E key. A window called
     File Explorer opens. This is where you look at your files.
  2. Find the Flightsleuth folder you were given and double-click it to
     open it. It is often under "Downloads" or "Documents" in the list on
     the left. If you cannot find it, ask the person who gave it to you
     where they put it.
  3. Find the file named "Start Flightsleuth" and double-click it
     (click it twice, quickly).

A window with the title "Flightsleuth" opens. That is the app.

The very first time you press Search, a small box asks for your key.
Click inside the box, hold the Ctrl key and press V to paste the key you
copied in Part 1, then click OK. It remembers the key from then on.
(If it never asks, the key was already saved for you.)


PART 3: SEARCHING FOR FLIGHTS
=============================

Fill in the window from top to bottom, then click the Search button.


From and To
-----------
Every airport has a three-letter code. You type the code, not the city
name. Some examples:

    JFK = New York (Kennedy)      LAX = Los Angeles
    ORD = Chicago (O'Hare)        MIA = Miami
    LHR = London (Heathrow)       CDG = Paris

To find any other code, search the web for the airport's name plus the
words "airport code".

If a city has several airports, you can list them with commas between,
like this:  JFK,EWR,LGA   This costs nothing extra and often finds a
cheaper flight.


Round trip or One way
---------------------
Click "Round trip" if you are going there and coming back.
Click "One way" if you are only going there.


Leave on, Come back on
----------------------
Each date has three little boxes: the month, the day, and the year.
Click a box and pick from the list that drops down.

"Come back on" turns grey when you choose One way, because it is not needed.


Also check ... days earlier and later (the most useful trick)
-------------------------------------------------------------
Flying a day or two earlier or later is often much cheaper. Put 2 in this
box and Flightsleuth checks 2 days before and 2 days after your dates too.

A bigger number checks more days, but uses up more of your 250 monthly
searches. 2 or 3 is a good choice. Leave it at 0 to check only your exact
dates.


Or search a whole range (when you do not mind which week you go)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Tick this box if any day in a long stretch would do, for example
"some time in March or April, for about a week".

  - "Leave on" becomes the FIRST day you could leave.
  - The date next to the tick box is the LAST day you could leave.
  - "staying 5 to 9 nights" is how long you want to be away. Change the
    two numbers to suit.

"Come back on" and "days earlier and later" turn grey, because the range
replaces them.

However long the range is, this never uses more than 25 searches. It
tries a spread of days first, then looks more closely around the cheapest
ones. It also confirms the best fare for you (see "Confirm the best
fare" below), so that box turns grey too.


Stops, Cabin, Adults, Carry-on bags
-----------------------------------
Stops           "Nonstop only" leaves out flights where you change planes.
Cabin           Economy is the normal, cheapest seat.
Adults          How many people are flying.
Carry-on bags   Put 1 here if you will bring a bag into the cabin. Every
                price then includes the bag, so cheap tickets that charge
                extra for it no longer look cheaper than they are.

You can leave all four alone.


Also check whether two one-way tickets are cheaper
--------------------------------------------------
Sometimes buying a ticket there and a separate ticket back costs less
than one round-trip ticket. Tick this box to check. It uses about three
times as many searches, so only tick it when you are close to booking.


Confirm the best fare
---------------------
The first price Flightsleuth shows for a round trip is the LISTED price.
Now and then a listed price turns out to have no flight home at that
price. Tick this box and Flightsleuth checks: it looks up the flight
home, finds out who sells the ticket, and compares a few other ways of
flying the same days.

It uses up to 6 more searches, so tick it when you have found dates you
like and are close to booking.


Search
------
Click Search. Next to the button it says "Searching... 2 of 5" while it
works. This takes a few seconds for each search. When it says "Done",
your results are below.

For a whole range, or with "Confirm the best fare" ticked, it says
"2 of up to 25" instead. It cannot know the exact number until the first
prices come back, so it tells you the most it could use. It often uses
fewer.


PART 4: READING THE RESULTS
===========================

The big bold line is the answer, for example:

    Cheapest listed: $455 round trip, Mon 11 Mar - Mon 18 Mar (not yet confirmed)

Under it, a line may say what Google thinks of that price, such as
"low" (a good deal) or "high" (more than usual for those dates).

The bold line tells you how far to trust the price:

    Cheapest listed: ... (not yet confirmed)
        This is what you see for a round trip unless you ticked "Confirm
        the best fare". It is normal. The price is listed, but nobody
        has looked up the flight home yet. Before you rely on it, either
        tick the box and search again, or click "Check this fare on
        Momondo", which uses none of your searches.

    Cheapest: ...
        The price was checked, flight home and all. A one-way ticket
        always says this, because it has no flight home to check.

    Cheapest confirmed: ...
        This price was checked. Cheaper ones are listed too, but nobody
        checked those.

    Cheapest listed: ... (not confirmed)
        Flightsleuth tried to check this price and could not. Be careful
        with it.


The two tabs
------------
Below the buttons are two tabs, like the tabs in a web browser. Click a
tab's name to see it.

    Prices                         the two lists described next
    Best fare, looked at closely   what "Confirm the best fare" found


Cheapest price for each date
----------------------------
The first list shows the lowest price for each pair of days. The row
shaded green is the cheapest.


Cheapest flights
----------------
The second list shows the actual flights, cheapest first.

    Fetched   when Flightsleuth got this price. If it says "(saved)",
              the price comes from an earlier search today and may have
              changed since. Every list has this column.
    Price     the total cost. For a round trip this covers going AND
              coming back.
    Route     which airports
    Outbound  the day and times of the flight going there. Times use the
              24-hour clock, so 18:30 means 6:30 in the evening.
              "+1" means it lands the next day.
    Return    the day you come back
    Stops     "nonstop" means no changing planes
    Time      how long the trip there takes
    Airline   who flies it


Best fare, looked at closely
----------------------------
This tab fills in when you ticked "Confirm the best fare" or searched a
whole range. At the top are the two flights, "out" and "back", with their
times and flight numbers. If a cheaper price was listed but had no flight
home, it says so here.

Under that is a list of other ways to fly the same days:

    Price     what it costs that way
    How       "Buy from ..." is who sells the same ticket.
              "Two one-way tickets" is a ticket there and a separate
              ticket back.
              "As sold in GB" (or another country) is the price shown to
              buyers in that country.
    Flights   "same flights" means the very same planes. "different
              itinerary" means other flights on the same days.
    Catch     anything to know before buying, such as:
              "two separate tickets"  if one flight is cancelled, the
                                      other airline owes you nothing
              "an agency, not the airline"  a travel website sells it
              "8h 35m layover"        a long wait between planes

A lower price with a catch is not always the better buy.


Check this fare on Momondo (do this first)
------------------------------------------
After a round-trip search, the first button under the bold line is
"Check this fare on Momondo". Momondo is a travel website that compares
many sellers. Click the button and your web browser opens Momondo on the
same airports and the same days.

Why first: in a test of 6 trips on 8 October 2026, Momondo showed the
same flights for less on 5 of them. The two largest savings, $97 and $51,
were on long trips. It is not always cheaper, so look before you decide.

On the Momondo page, find the same flights (the same airline and the same
times) and compare the price with the one Flightsleuth showed.

If the bold line says "not yet confirmed" or "not confirmed", this is
also a free way to confirm the price. It uses none of your searches. If
Momondo shows those flights at about that price, that is a good sign the
price is real.

Momondo opens for 1 adult in Economy with any number of stops. If you
searched for something else, the line under the buttons says what to set
on the Momondo page first, so that you compare like with like.

A one-way search has no such button. Momondo is then one of the "Same
dates on:" buttons described further down.

If clicking a deal on Momondo does nothing
    When this was written (October 2026), the deal buttons on Momondo did
    nothing in the Firefox browser on the computer of the person who made
    Flightsleuth. They worked in the Chrome browser. If the same happens
    to you:

      1. In Flightsleuth, click "Copy the address".
      2. Open Chrome, or any other browser you have.
      3. Click in the address bar at the top, hold the Ctrl key and
         press V to paste, then press Enter.

Save the price you found there (you can skip this)
    Click "Save the price you found there...". A small window opens.
    Type the price you saw on Momondo. If you know them, also fill in who
    sells the ticket at that price, whether it is the same flights, and
    whether the seller's payment page still showed that price. Only the
    price is needed. Click Save.

    Flightsleuth writes this down on your own computer, next to the price
    it found itself, and says under the buttons which was cheaper and by
    how much. Over time that shows how much checking Momondo really
    saves. It uses none of your searches and sends nothing anywhere.


Watch this fare (be told when it gets cheaper)
----------------------------------------------
Next to the Search button is a button named "Watch this fare...". It is
grey until a search has found a flight.

A watch keeps an eye on the cheapest price for the days you searched.
Once a day, or less often if you choose, Flightsleuth looks the price up
again. If it has dropped, a message arrives on your phone.

Two things have to be set up once on this computer before that works:
the messages to your phone, and the daily check. Both are done by typing
commands. The file named README explains them under "Watches and
alerts". If somebody set Flightsleuth up for you, ask them whether they
did this. Until it is done a watch is saved but nothing checks it, and
the line next to the buttons says so.

To add a watch:

  1. Search for the trip as usual.
  2. Click "Watch this fare...". A small window opens. Its first lines
     name the days and the price you are looking at.
  3. Say what you want to be told about. You can leave it all as it is.

     "Tell me when it is below this price"
         Type a price, like 450. Leave it empty if you have none in mind.

     "or this many percent under its 30-day median"
         The median is the middle price of the last 30 days. 15 means:
         tell me when it is 15 percent cheaper than that. 0 turns this
         off.

     "or below Google's typical range for these dates"
         Google says what these days usually cost. Leave this ticked to
         be told when the price is under that.

     "Check it every this many days"
         1 means every day. A bigger number uses fewer of your searches.

  4. Click Watch.

Each check uses 1 of your searches, and up to 3 more on a day when the
price is worth telling you about. Adding the watch uses none. You can
have 3 watches at most.

If your searches cannot cover the watch, a message says so and shows the
sums. It also says how many days between checks would fit. Change the
last box to that number and click Watch again.

When the price drops, your phone shows the trip, the new price, why you
are being told, and the flights. Tap the message and Momondo opens on
those days. You are told once for each price. A second message comes only
if it drops again, by at least $5 and at least 2 percent.

Flightsleuth looks up the return flights before it tells you, so a price
on your phone is one whose flights both ways were found. It is still a
price from a few minutes ago: check it on the page before you pay.

A watch stops by itself once the day you would leave has passed. Seeing
your watches, and removing one, is done by typing a command. The README
explains how.


Booking the flight
------------------
Flightsleuth only FINDS flights. It does not buy them.

To book, click the "Check and book" button under the bold line. Your web
browser opens on a page showing the same flights, where you can choose
your return flight and buy the ticket.

Always check the price on that page before you pay. Prices change often,
and the one Flightsleuth showed may be a few hours old.

The buttons after "Same dates on:" open the same days on other travel
websites, in case one of them is cheaper. Two of them carry a warning in
their name:

    "lists hidden-city fares"     tickets where you get off at a stop
                                  and skip the last flight. Airlines
                                  forbid this, and you cannot check a bag.
    "lists self-transfer fares"   tickets where you change planes on your
                                  own, collecting and re-checking your
                                  bags. If the first flight is late,
                                  nobody rebooks you.

If you are not sure what those mean, stay with "Check and book".


PART 5: IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
===============================

A message box appears when there is a problem. Click OK, fix the problem,
and press Search again.

"... is not an airport list like JFK"
    You typed a city name or misspelled a code. Use the three-letter code.

"Fill in both the From and To airports"
    One of the two boxes at the top is empty.

"... is not a real date"
    You picked a day that month does not have, like February 31.

"The departure date is in the past"
    The Leave on date has already happened. Check the year.

"The return date is before the departure date"
    Come back on must be the same day as Leave on, or later.

"The date window ends before it starts"
    You ticked "search a whole range", and the last day you could leave
    is earlier than "Leave on". Swap them, or check the year.

"The most nights is fewer than the fewest nights"
    The two "staying" numbers are the wrong way round. Put the smaller
    one first.

"That would use ... searches at once, and the limit is 25"
    You asked it to check too much in one go. Use a smaller number of
    days earlier and later, or untick the one-way tickets box or the
    "Confirm the best fare" box. Or tick "search a whole range", which
    always stays inside the limit.

"... only ... are left this month"
    You have used up your free searches. They refill next month.

"Invalid API key"
    The key was not pasted correctly. Click the "SerpApi key..." button
    next to Search, paste the key again, and click OK.

"Network error"
    The computer is not connected to the internet. Check your connection
    and try again.

"No fares found"
    No flights matched. Try different days, or set Stops back to
    "Any number of stops".

"The searches cannot cover this watch"
    Checking this watch as often as you asked would use more searches
    than you have. The message shows the sums and says how many days
    between checks would fit. Put that number in the last box.

"There are already 3 watches, which is the most"
    One has to be removed before another can be added. That is done by
    typing a command; the README explains how.

"A watch needs at least one rule"
    You emptied the price, set the percent to 0 and unticked the box, so
    there is nothing left to tell you about. Fill in one of the three.

"Watching it, but alerts are not set up"
    The watch is saved, but this computer cannot message your phone yet.
    See "Watches and alerts" in the README, or ask the person who set
    Flightsleuth up for you.

Nothing opens when you double-click "Start Flightsleuth", or Windows asks
which program to open it with
    The computer is missing a free program called Python that Flightsleuth
    needs. Ask the person who gave you this app to install it for you.


QUICK REMINDER CARD
===================

  1. Windows key + E, then open the Flightsleuth folder you were given
  2. Double-click "Start Flightsleuth"
  3. Type the airport codes, pick the dates, click Search
  4. Click "Check this fare on Momondo" to see if it is cheaper there
  5. Click "Check and book" to buy the flight in your web browser
  6. Not ready to buy? Click "Watch this fare..." to be told on your
     phone if it gets cheaper (this needs setting up once; see above)

(There is also a way to run searches by typing commands. It is described
in the file named README, for people comfortable with that.)
